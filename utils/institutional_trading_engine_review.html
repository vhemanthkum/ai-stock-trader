Verdict — what I actually think of your engine

What works

Architecture is genuinely solid

The separation of data collection → agent analysis → chairman synthesis is correct institutional design. The async parallel agent calls per stock, the retry logic pattern, the SQLite logging, the macro regime gate — these are real concepts done right. Most retail quant projects I've seen don't get this far. You have a working skeleton.

Critical flaw 1

The screener is toy math, not institutional screening

Volume surge +30 pts, price breakout +25 pts, oversold drop +20 pts — this is momentum chasing with arbitrary weights, not a screening system. The fundamental overlay is a scoring afterthought, not a gate. An institutional screener eliminates first, ranks second. Your system ranks without eliminating, which means garbage stocks with high momentum scores (illiquid microcaps, stocks with bloated volume from operator activity, stocks at 52-week lows on bad earnings) pass into your council's queue. The LLMs then waste tokens on stocks that should never have gotten through.

Critical flaw 2

Fundamental agent using llama-3.3-70b-versatile is wrong

You assigned fundamental analysis — the most complex reasoning task involving 5-year financial history, promoter pledging, forensic accounting signals, ROCE trajectory — to Groq's llama-3.3-70b-versatile with a 4096 token limit. This model will confidently confabulate numbers it doesn't have. The Chairman on Gemini Flash is worse. Neither has actual access to Screener.in, BSE filings, or concall transcripts. They are reasoning over whatever you pass in the prompt — so your output quality is bounded entirely by the quality of your data pipeline, which I cannot verify from the files you shared.

Structural problem 3

API fallback chain will silently degrade quality

When Groq 429s and falls back to Gemini Flash, the output format and reasoning depth change. You are not flagging this degradation in your output. A BUY call from llama-3.3-70b-versatile and a BUY call from llama-3.1-8b-instant (after fallback) are not the same confidence level. The report will show them identically. An institutional system marks the model that produced each call and weights confidence scores accordingly.

Structural problem 4

NVIDIA DeepSeek R1 allocation is missing from actual code

config.py says NVIDIA_BASE_URL is set, test_nvidia.py exists — but the AGENT_MODELS dict assigns fundamental to groq / llama-3.3-70b-versatile, not NVIDIA. Your design doc says A3 = DeepSeek R1 on NVIDIA GPU infrastructure. That's not implemented. Worth verifying whether your NVIDIA key is actually being routed anywhere.

Structural problem 5

4-second flat throttle is both wasteful and insufficient

You sleep 4.0 seconds between every stock regardless of which provider just responded. If Groq replied in 200ms, you waste 3.8 seconds. If Google is having a slow day and replied in 3.9s, you're sending the next call 100ms later — right into the next rate window. You need per-provider rate tracking with a token bucket or sliding window counter, not a flat sleep.

Bottom line

It will produce output. The question is whether to trust it.

The engine will run, call APIs, produce BUY/HOLD/AVOID calls, and generate HTML reports. But as it stands, the screener doesn't eliminate low-quality candidates properly, the models are not matched to the task depth, and the output confidence scores have no calibration against historical calls. You cannot use this for real capital without a backtest layer and a screener overhaul. Fix the screener first — everything downstream gets better automatically.

How I would personally pick and trade — 20 years institutional lens

This is how the engine should think, not just what rules to follow.

The philosophy first

I look for three things aligning simultaneously: a business I understand, a price that makes sense, and a market environment where being long is not fighting gravity. When all three align, I size up. When any one is absent, I wait. Most of the time, the right answer is wait. The biggest mistake your engine makes right now is it tries to produce BUY calls every day. Markets give you maybe 40–50 good setups per year per sector. Your job is to catch those 40–50, not manufacture 15 per day.

My personal stock selection hierarchy

1

Market regime first — always

If Nifty is below 200-DMA and India VIX is above 20, I do not go long anything regardless of how good the stock looks. I either go to cash or short via index puts. Your engine has a VIX gate at 17 and 22 — that is directionally right, worth keeping. But it needs to also check Nifty vs. 200-DMA. Many bad days in 2022 and 2024 had VIX under 17 while Nifty was clearly in downtrend.

2

Sector rotation second — before individual stocks

Which 2–3 sectors are showing the best relative strength vs. Nifty 500 this month? Only go hunting in those sectors. Trying to pick a winning stock in a weak sector is hard mode — you fight the macro headwind. Your sector agent exists but is not upstream enough. Sector rotation should be the input that determines which part of your 500-stock universe you even look at, cutting it to maybe 100–150 before any other filter.

3

Quality gate — non-negotiable eliminators

I will not touch a company where: promoter has pledged more than 15% shares, auditor has changed in last 2 years without explanation, operating cash flow is negative for 2+ consecutive years, gross block additions don't match claimed capex (forensic signal), or revenue recognition is unusually front-loaded in Q4. These are red flags that no volume surge or momentum score overrides.

4

Setup type — what am I actually trading?

I need to know before entry: is this a momentum breakout (price near 52-wk high, volume confirming), a mean reversion play (fundamentally strong stock oversold on market fear, not company news), or a catalyst play (earnings, order win, sector policy). Each setup has different entry, stop, and target logic. Your chairman agent currently tries to do all three simultaneously in one prompt — that's too vague. Force it to classify setup type first, then apply setup-specific rules.

5

Entry precision — not ranges, actual levels

Your engine produces entry_range_low to entry_range_high. That's fine for output display. But internally, the agent should produce a specific entry trigger: "buy on a close above ₹1,247 with volume > 1.8× average" or "buy on a retest of the 50-DMA if it holds above ₹1,190". A ₹50 entry range means nothing actionable. The model should cite a specific technical level it is watching.

6

Stop-loss — ATR-based, not percentage

Your config shows DEFAULT_STOP_LOSS_PCT = 7.0 and ATR_STOP_MULTIPLIER = 2.0. The ATR stop is correct. The 7% flat stop is wrong — for a volatile stock with ATR of ₹85, a 7% stop on a ₹500 stock (₹35 = 7%) would be hit by normal intraday noise. Stop = entry price minus 2×ATR(14). No exceptions. If that gives you a stop that risks more than 1% of portfolio on a 0.5% position size rule, the position is too large — scale it down, don't move the stop.

7

Target — asymmetric, staged

Minimum 2:1 reward:risk before entry. Preferred 3:1. Target 1 = 1× ATR above breakout level (take 40% off). Target 2 = previous resistance / measured move (take 40% off). Let 20% run with trailing stop at 10-DMA. This staged exit removes the psychology problem of "when do I sell." Your engine shows target_1 and target_2 — good structure, but they need to be calculated from ATR and resistance levels, not just percentages.

Problem 3: Rate limit tracking is naive

You track limits only via static config constants and flat sleep delays. You need a per-provider rate tracker that counts actual requests made this minute and this day, and implements token bucket logic. Here's the minimal correct pattern:

class RateLimiter:
    def __init__(self, rpm: int, rpd: int):
        self.rpm = rpm
        self.rpd = rpd
        self.minute_calls = []
        self.day_calls = 0

    async def acquire(self):
        now = time.time()
        # Clear calls older than 60 seconds
        self.minute_calls = [t for t in self.minute_calls if now - t < 60]
        if len(self.minute_calls) >= self.rpm:
            wait = 60 - (now - self.minute_calls[0])
            await asyncio.sleep(wait + 0.1)
        if self.day_calls >= self.rpd:
            raise Exception("Daily limit reached — switch to fallback")
        self.minute_calls.append(now)
        self.day_calls += 1

One instance per provider, injected into each agent. This replaces all your static API_DELAY constants.

def screen_nifty500(top_n=30):
    # --- STAGE 1A: HARD GATES (boolean, no scoring) ---
    survivors = []
    for stock in nifty500_universe:
        data = yf.Ticker(stock)
        info = data.fast_info

        # Gate 1: Market cap
        if info.market_cap < 500_00_00_000:  # < ₹500 Cr
            continue
        # Gate 2: Liquidity (20-day avg volume)
        hist = data.history(period="30d")
        avg_vol = hist['Volume'].tail(20).mean()
        if avg_vol < 200_000:  # < 2L shares
            continue
        # Gate 3: Trend (above 200-DMA)
        hist_1y = data.history(period="1y")
        dma200 = hist_1y['Close'].tail(200).mean()
        if hist_1y['Close'].iloc[-1] < dma200:
            continue
        # Gate 4: Debt check (skip if D/E unavailable)
        de = info.get('debtToEquity', 0)
        if de and de > 150:  # yfinance returns D/E as %, 150 = 1.5x
            continue
        survivors.append(stock)

    # --- STAGE 1B: SCORE survivors only ---
    scored = []
    for stock in survivors:
        score = compute_institutional_score(stock)
        sector_rs = get_sector_rs(stock)
        adjusted = score * sector_rs  # sector multiplier
        scored.append((stock, adjusted))

    scored.sort(key=lambda x: x[1], reverse=True)
    return [s[0] for s in scored[:top_n]]
